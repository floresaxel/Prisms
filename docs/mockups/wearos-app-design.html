Prisms

Wear OS — 25 directions

Twenty-five takes on the same deliberately small watch app: show today's itinerary and add a task. Same day and same new task in all twenty-five, so the differences are design and nothing else. Each direction is a pair — the itinerary screen and its add-task screen. Frames are Pixel Watch 3 45 mm (228 × 228 dp) drawn at 1.5×; project colours are the Prisms tones from apps/mobile/src/ui.tsx, sitting on Material 3 dark surfaces.

In scope

  • Today's scheduled items
  • Time · title · project colour
  • Done / now / upcoming
  • Tap to complete
  • Add a task
  • Optional project + duration

Deliberately left on the phone

  • Clock-in / timers
  • Focus review
  • Journal & day notes
  • Day-map drag
  • Automations
  • Review inbox
  • Project tree
  • Habits management

What makes these Wear OS and not watchOS

Round screen
No corners. A full-width row loses its ends at the top and bottom of the list, which is why so many directions below curve, centre or inscribe their content.
ScalingLazyColumn
The platform list scales and fades items as they approach the edge, and centres on the middle item instead of top-aligning. Rendered here with the .s95–.s68 classes.
Curved TimeText
The clock is drawn along the top arc, not sat in a corner. Real SVG textPath, so the glyphs actually rotate.
Position indicator
The scrollbar is a short arc on the right edge, tracking the crown.
Chips, not rows
Material's stadium-shaped chip — leading circle, primary and secondary label — is the native list item.
Tiles & complications
Swipe-reachable surfaces that exist before the app is launched. Directions 07 and 25 live there.

01

Material Chips

The platform default

Exactly what Wear OS ships: stadium chips with a leading circle, primary and secondary label, in a ScalingLazyColumn that centres on the middle item. Curved TimeText up top, curved position indicator on the right. The add action is a primary-container chip at the end of the list — Material's own pattern for "the affirmative thing in this list".

02

Scaling Column

Lean into the curve

The same list, but the scaling is pushed hard: the focused item is full size and its neighbours fall away fast. The circle stops being a container the list is fighting and becomes a lens the list is read through. The crown drives it, so the whole day is reachable without a finger on the glass.

One answer, centred

04

Radial Dial

The screen already is a clock

A 12-hour dial with an arc per block and a hand at the current time. On a round watch this needs no metaphor at all — the display's shape and the data's shape are the same shape. Adding means turning the bezel or crown to a free wedge, which the dial draws as an outlined gap.

05

Rotating Bezel

Galaxy Watch

Built for the one input Wear OS has that watchOS doesn't: Samsung's physical rotating bezel. Items sit as pips around the rim, the selection marker is fixed at twelve o'clock, and turning the bezel spins the day past it with a detent per item. Nothing ever hides under your finger.

06

Curved Text

Wear's own typography

Wear OS is the only watch platform with a real curved-text primitive, and this direction spends it: the next item arcs under the clock, the action arcs along the bottom rim, and the middle is left entirely to the thing happening now. The chrome moves to the edge so the content can own the centre.

Tiles

Before the app exists

Not an app screen at all — a pair of Tiles, the surface you reach by swiping sideways from the watch face. Zero launches, zero navigation: one swipe shows the day, one more offers four one-tap tasks. Tiles can't scroll, so the design is forced to decide what the three most important rows are.

08

Edge-Hug Buttons

Material 3 Expressive

The newest Wear OS idiom: controls that press flat against the rim, letting the circular clip cut their outer edge into an arc. It reclaims the top and bottom of the display, which every other layout surrenders to the curve, and puts the add button exactly where the thumb already rests.

Concentric Rings

A lane per project

Four nested rings, one per project, each carrying that project's blocks as arcs on a 12-hour dial. A single red hand crosses all four. It answers a question no list can: which parts of your life are actually getting time today — an empty ring is as loud as a full one.

Speak it, don't type it

Distance is time

Always-On

The state you're in 95% of the time

Designs for ambient mode first. Wear OS's always-on display allows only a small fraction of lit pixels and refreshes once a minute, so this is outline dots, dimmed type and no fills — but it means your next three items are on your wrist without ever raising it. Full colour returns on the tap.

Thumb Arc

Reachability

A watch is operated by one thumb arriving from below, and the bottom arc is the only part of a round screen it reaches without the wrist rolling.
